Good Actor (Ethical Use) 
When performed by a good actor, such as a network administrator or a cybersecurity professional, 
sniffing is used to ensure network security and integrity. Here are some attacks that can be detected 
through ethical sniffing: 
Intrusion Attempts: Unusual patterns in network traffic can indicate an attempt to breach the network. 
Early detection can prevent unauthorized access. 
Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) Attacks: A sudden influx of traffic from multiple sources can be a 
sign of a DDoS attack aimed at overwhelming and incapacitating a network or service. 
Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attacks: Sniffing can help detect anomalies that suggest the presence of an 
unauthorized entity intercepting and possibly altering communications between two parties. 
Network Misconfigurations: Unintended network traffic flows can indicate misconfigurations that could 
lead to vulnerabilities. 
Malware Communication: Traffic patterns related to known malware signatures can be identified, 
indicating a compromised system within the network. 
Detecting these types of attacks enables organizations to respond swiftly to mitigate potential damage, 
such as data breaches, service downtime, or compromised sensitive information.

Bad Actor (Malicious Use) 
Conversely, when performed by a bad actor, sniffing is a technique used to intercept and analyze 
network traffic for malicious purposes. Here's what can be gleaned and done with this information: 
Eavesdropping on Communications: Capturing sensitive information such as login credentials, personal 
data, or confidential corporate information. 
Session Hijacking: By capturing session tokens or cookies, a bad actor can impersonate a legitimate user 
on a web service, gaining unauthorized access. 
Network Mapping: Determining the network's structure, identifying active devices and services, and 
planning further attacks. 
Man-in-the-Middle Attacks: By intercepting and possibly altering the communications between two 
parties without their knowledge, leading to data breaches or misinformation. 
Spread of Malware: Using captured information to tailor phishing or spear-phishing attacks, leading to 
malware installation on targeted systems. 
 
Importance of Cybersecurity 
This dual perspective on sniffing highlights the critical importance of cybersecurity measures within an 
organization. Proactive measures, such as encrypting sensitive data, regularly monitoring and analyzing 
network traffic, implementing strong access controls, and educating employees about security best 
practices, can significantly reduce the risk of both internal and external attacks. 
Investing in cybersecurity is not just about protecting information; it's about safeguarding an 
organization's reputation, ensuring the continuity of operations, and complying with regulatory 
requirements. In today's digital age, where data breaches can have monumental financial and 
reputational repercussions, prioritizing cybersecurity is not an option but a necessity.
